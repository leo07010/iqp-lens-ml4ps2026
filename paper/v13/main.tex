\begin{abstract}
Instantaneous-quantum-polynomial (IQP) circuits are believed hard to sample
classically~\cite{bremner_2011_classical,bremner_2016_average}, yet the expectation values of a
maximum-mean-discrepancy (MMD) loss stay computable, so an IQP Born machine can be trained
classically and deployed on quantum hardware~\cite{recio_2025_train}. A recent
correlation--complexity map is proposed to indicate which data suit such models, and DeepLense
strong-lensing images sit at its most IQP-compatible position among the encodings we measured. On
these images the reference protocol, a core frozen on one anchor followed by sequential per-image
adaptation, generates noise. Joint training repairs it. A learned linear latent map then loses
coherence past $M{=}6$, so a small non-linear map carries the latent instead. With eleven training
images the resulting 10-qubit machine matches a DCGAN with six times as many parameters on both
generation metrics within the seed spread. It leads the DCGAN on a flux-aligned variant, leads an RBM
on the two MMD$^2$ columns, is level with the RBM on the histogram metric, and projects unseen
images at correlation $0.901$. With $100$ images the DCGAN leads and the correlation reaches
$0.977$. All circuits are simulated exactly at $n=10$ qubits over five seeds, so we claim no
quantum advantage and treat the work as an early test of whether such a model could be worth
deploying where training data are scarce.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

IQP circuits are commuting-gate circuits whose output distribution is the squared
Walsh--Hadamard~\cite{walsh_1923_closed} transform of a phase function. Exact or multiplicatively
approximate classical sampling from them would collapse the polynomial
hierarchy~\cite{bremner_2011_classical}, and the hardness is conjectured to hold on average under
additive error~\cite{bremner_2016_average}. The motivation has limits. Noise makes such circuits
simulable in polynomial time~\cite{rajakumar_2024_polynomial}, classical algorithms keep
improving~\cite{maslov_2024_fast}, and trainability itself pulls towards
simulability~\cite{cerezo_2025_does}. The same structure keeps the expectation values of an MMD
loss computable~\cite{recio_2025_iqpopt}, as for other encodings built to stay
simulable~\cite{lizziobosco_2026_pauli}. Such a model can therefore be trained classically and
deployed on quantum hardware~\cite{recio_2025_train}, following earlier Born machines trained with
an MMD loss~\cite{liu_2018_differentiable,benedetti_2019_generative,coyle_2020_born}.

Whether real data sit where this helps is an empirical question. A recent
proposal~\cite{liu_2026_correlation} approaches it with a \emph{correlation--complexity map}. The map
places a dataset at $\qcli$, the Jensen--Shannon divergence between its Walsh order spectrum (the
Fourier power at each Hamming weight) and the i.i.d.\ binomial baseline, and at $\cci \in [0,1]$,
the share of its correlation that the best pairwise tree
misses~\cite{watanabe_1960_information,schneidman_2003_network,chow_1968_approximating}. IQP
circuits should do well in the high-$\qcli$, high-$\cci$ corner. The same paper gives a
latent-adaptation protocol (Algorithm~1 of its v2), in which a phase core is trained on one anchor
and a block of the first $50$ phases in Hamming order is randomly initialised and adapted sample by
sample. It was demonstrated at $n=18$ qubits on 2D turbulence~\cite{khojasteh_2022_lagrangian}, one
of several datasets the map covers. A follow-up initialises the same fixed block from a
physics-informed surrogate~\cite{liu_2026_physics}, whereas we learn the directions.

We apply both to simulated dark-matter strong-lensing images from
DeepLense~\cite{alexander_2020_deep,alexander_2021_decoding}. Lens crops land at the most
IQP-compatible map position we measured, and a permutation null shows that their $\qcli$ is largely set
by the intensity histogram, so that position is not by itself evidence of IQP-suitable structure. On static images the reference latent protocol produces noise. We
trace the cause and replace its latent map by a non-linear one, then compare the model with an RBM
and a DCGAN under two intensity conventions. App.~\ref{app:turb} reports a replication on the
turbulence data.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Four choices differ from the reference setup, each following from the data. The reference snapshots interpolate to unseen times,
while the DeepLense \texttt{Model~II} images we use~\cite{ml4sci_deeplense} are static, so we
generate unconditionally and project onto held-out images. Its 18-bit $(x,y,\mathrm{intensity})$
encoding suits a field of tuples, but the raw intensity axis of a lens crop carries barely one bit.
We instead centroid-align each image and read its central $32\times32$ crop, which holds $99.4\%$
of the light, as one probability mass function (pmf) over $1{,}024$ cells at $n=10$
(App.~\ref{app:pipe}). The reference fixes the latent block, whereas we learn the map and train the
$N$ images jointly. It also estimates its MMD from samples, while a $10$-qubit pmf is exact.

Every arm is a $10$-qubit IQP Born machine over $1{,}024$ Walsh phases $\theta$, one per qubit
subset. The empty subset is a global phase, which leaves $1{,}023$ effective. The arms differ only
in how a per-image latent enters. The reference protocol freezes the first $50$ phases in Hamming
order and adapts them sample by sample. On static crops this reconstructs the other training images
poorly (Sec.~\ref{sec:latent}), so we make the map trainable, first as a linear map of $M$
directions,
\begin{equation}
\theta_i \;=\; \theta^{0} + \Phi^{\!\top} z_i,
\qquad \theta^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{1024},\quad
\Phi\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times 1024},\quad z_i \in \mathbb{R}^{M},
\label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
with each row of $\Phi$ normalised in $L^2$. On these crops the linear map degrades by $M{=}6$. We
suspect the $\|z\|$ the images demand exceeds the radius over which the Walsh phases interfere
coherently, but we have not isolated the cause. The main arm replaces $\Phi^{\!\top} z$ by a
one-hidden-layer $\tanh$ map $g$ of width $H{=}32$, with the $z_i$ trained jointly with $g$ under a
small $L^2$ penalty. Its objective is the flat MMD (uniform Walsh weights, estimable from hardware
shots). The three-bandwidth Gaussian kernel of~\cite{recio_2025_train}, the \emph{paper kernel} of
the tables, is kept as a secondary arm. $\theta^{0}$ is warm-started by $1{,}000$ exact-KL steps.

The trained pmf fixes only the relative intensities. We paste it into a $64\times64$ frame at a
total intensity $S$ and at an offset drawn from the offsets of the training crops. The reference
convention gives every frame the training-set mean $S$, so the generated flux has zero variance
while real crops spread by about a quarter of their mean. We therefore draw $S$ per image from a
kernel density estimate of the $N$ training totals, with no retraining, and App.~\ref{app:fixedS}
repeats everything under the fixed $S$. The baselines are a restricted Boltzmann machine
(RBM)~\cite{smolensky_1986_information,hinton_2002_training,tieleman_2008_training} and a deep
convolutional generative adversarial network (DCGAN)~\cite{radford_2015_unsupervised}. Both are
early-stopped on $200$ validation images, while the IQP arms run a fixed $4{,}000$ steps, which can
only favour the baselines. PDF--JS is the Jensen--Shannon divergence between intensity histograms.
Conv2D-MMD$^2$ is an MMD in the feature space of a fixed random
encoder~\cite{gretton_2012_kernel,binkowski_2018_demystifying}. The arms set flux in different
ways, so we add a post-hoc flux-aligned Conv2D-MMD$^2$ that rescales every frame to the held-out
mean total. For unseen images we report the projection correlation, taking the best of eight latent
restarts by the fit's own loss. Bins, scale and bandwidth are set on the held-out data alone, so no
metric moves with the arm scored. Every arm generates $200$ images against the same $200$ held-out
frames at $N \in \{11,100\}$. Each number is a mean $\pm$ sample sd over five seeds, shown beside a
real-vs-real floor between two disjoint real sets. The $N$ training images are fixed across seeds,
so the spread is initialisation and sampling variance alone. A lead in sd is the difference of two
means in units of the baseline's seed sd, a descriptive quantity and not a significance test.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Where lens crops sit on the correlation--complexity map}
\label{sec:map}

Table~\ref{tab:map} recomputes the map (Fig.~\ref{fig:map}(a)). Under the encoding the reference
paper uses for turbulence, lens images sit far above turbulence in $\qcli$ and inside the corner
the map calls IQP-compatible. A permutation null that keeps each image's intensity histogram comes
within $14\%$ of the lens value, and within a factor of two of the much smaller turbulence value.
Under this encoding the index therefore mostly tracks the histogram. Both indices depend on $K$, the
number of codes per image, so each row should be read against its own-$K$ null. At the protocol's
own $N_t = 5\times10^{4}$ samples per snapshot~\cite{liu_2026_correlation} the turbulence $\qcli$
falls to $0.091$, against a null of $0.098$. The 10-bit crop pmf, which is the encoding we model,
separates from its null in both coordinates. It sits at $\cci = 0.43$, outside the high-$\cci$
corner, so its high $\qcli$ motivates the encoding but gives no reason to prefer an IQP over a
pairwise model.

\subsection{From the reference protocol to a non-linear latent map}
\label{sec:latent}

Run sequentially on our own $50$-phase block (App.~\ref{app:arms}), Algorithm~1 produces noise
(Fig.~\ref{fig:map}(b), second row). The circuit can represent the data, since it fits a
\emph{single} aligned crop to correlation $0.99997 \pm 0.00001$. The block is not the cause either, since
training the same $50$ phases jointly with the core across all $N$ images (the \emph{fixed basis,
joint} row of Table~\ref{tab:main}) recovers most of the gap. The failure lies in freezing the core
on one anchor and adapting each image in sequence. With the core frozen, the $50$ fixed coordinates
reconstruct the other training images at only $0.44 \pm 0.04$. We did not sweep the block size of
$50$. Equation~\eqref{eq:affine} closes the rest of the gap but has a width limit of its own. On a
$20$-image sweep (Table~\ref{tab:msweep}) the held-out projection correlation of the learned linear
map falls steeply from $M{=}2$ to $M{=}8$. The non-linear map avoids that collapse and stays slightly
above the nearest-training-image reference. The non-linear
map is flat to $M{=}4$ and degrades from $M{=}8$, so it does not buy a large useful width. At
$M{=}2$, where we report it, its held-out projection correlation is $0.901$ at $N{=}11$ and $0.977$
at $N{=}100$. Projecting the nearest training image gives $0.892$ and $0.970$, and a single
zero-initialised restart gives $0.838$ and $0.887$.

The IQP arms are competitive in the eleven-image regime of Table~\ref{tab:main}. There the
non-linear arm matches the DCGAN on PDF--JS and raw Conv2D-MMD$^2$ within seed noise and leads on
the flux-aligned column. It leads the RBM on the two MMD$^2$ columns and is within one sd on PDF--JS
(last two rows of Table~\ref{tab:main}), with a sixth of the DCGAN's parameters and three times the
RBM's. Under the paper kernel the same map has the
lowest mean PDF--JS, $0.75 \times 10^{-3}$ against $1.1 \pm 0.6$ for the DCGAN, still within one sd,
and a worse flux-aligned column. At neither size does an arm lead the DCGAN on all three columns by
more than a seed sd. At $100$ images the DCGAN leads on PDF--JS and raw Conv2D-MMD$^2$, and the
flux-aligned $1.8 \pm 0.2$ sits within one sd of its $2.1 \pm 0.4$.

The flux-aligned column does not depend on the intensity convention, and the largest paired
difference over the $325$ runs carrying both is $6\times10^{-9}$. Invariance is not independence, and the two MMD$^2$ columns
overlap, since the aligned one measures shape and the raw one measures shape plus flux. The
sampling rule also treats the arms unequally. At $N{=}11$ it improves the raw Conv2D-MMD$^2$ of
every IQP arm by $22$ to $39\%$ and makes the RBM's $20\%$ worse, and under the fixed intensity the
RBM leads the non-linear arm on that column ($6.5 \pm 0.8$ against $8.5 \pm 0.2$,
App.~\ref{app:fixedS}). The raw-column ranking against the RBM is therefore a property of the
sampled decode and not of the model. Sampling reproduces the spread of the training totals but keeps
their mean, and the eleven training frames are $9\%$ dimmer than the held-out set. That offset is why
the raw column of the learned-map IQP arms at $N{=}11$ lands level with the DCGAN and not near the floor. App.~\ref{app:sweeps} reports data scaling over $N \in \{10,\dots,100\}$ and per-class
retraining.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:disc}

The competitive standing of the $10$-qubit model at eleven images does not carry over to $100$ images. A
deployed margin would also be bounded by connectivity and noise~\cite{placidi_2026_connectivity}.
The results concern sample quality and projection fidelity alone and do not show that a quantum device would beat a classical
one on this task.
Generated frames sit farther in $L_2$ from their nearest training image than real held-out frames
do ($4.21 \pm 0.10$ against $2.90$ at $N{=}11$), so the arm does not memorise. Two steps cannot be
estimated from shots as run. The exact-KL warm start can be replaced within one seed sd
(App.~\ref{app:arms}), but the projection optimises $z$ against the exact pmf. At $n=10$ we
parameterise all $2^{n}$ Walsh phases, and scaling up would need them
sparsified~\cite{liu_2026_correlation}, which we have not tested. Only the model fit sees $N$
images. Bandwidths, floors and the baselines' early stopping use hundreds to thousands of real
frames, so the study limits the training set and not the pipeline. Alignment and the crop leave
translation and the outer $0.6\%$ of the light outside the task, and the floor shares this
restriction. Five seeds are few. The DCGAN alone is not bit-reproducible and adds up to $57\%$ of
spread at one seed. The sampled intensity moves the raw column unequally across arms. Everything rests on one simulated dataset, the metrics do not test
substructure, and no destroyed image is scored as an upper reference.

\section{Turbulence replication, training and evaluation details}
\label{app:turb}

On that paper's own dataset (2D cylinder-wake turbulence D2~\cite{khojasteh_2022_lagrangian},
$n=18$ qubits, 11 anchor snapshots, five seeds) we do not reproduce the result under our
pre-registered convention. The RBM (PDF--JS $0.217 \pm 0.007$) and a 1.2M-parameter DCGAN
($0.045 \pm 0.062$) both score better than our 31k-parameter IQP ($0.272 \pm 0.004$) on both
metrics. Under last-write-wins aggregation, which that paper~\cite{liu_2026_correlation} never
specifies, our PDF--JS moves towards the published values without reaching them ($0.0324$ and
$0.0473$ against $0.0238$ and $0.0221$), and the RBM still leads. On these data Conv2D-MMD$^2$ does
not rank a destroyed image below a trained one. Pixel-shuffled ground truth scores
$0.7045 \pm 0.0095$, better than every model in that paper's Table~2. That paper itself notes that
its baselines are not exhaustive and that this metric does not replace spectral
diagnostics~\cite{liu_2026_correlation}.

The $^{\dagger}$ arm runs Algorithm~1 of v2~\cite{liu_2026_correlation} on our own latent block.
That block holds the $50$ subsets of Hamming weight at most $2$ whose Walsh coefficients vary most
over a disjoint pool of $2{,}000$ images, where the reference block is the first $50$ in Hamming
order. \emph{Fixed basis, joint} trains that block jointly with the core across all $N$ images.
Params are totals at that $N$, counting shared parameters ($\theta^{0}$ included) plus $N$
per-image latents. The fixed-basis arms hold $974 + 50N$, the learned-direction arms
$5{,}120 + MN$ and the RBM $10{,}960 + 50N$. The DCGAN entry counts its generator alone, and its
discriminator adds $53{,}497$. The RBM has $10$ visible and $1{,}000$ hidden units on the $10$-bit
code. Each image owns its visible biases and the first $40$ hidden biases, and the RBM ran $160$k
or $80$k steps. The DCGAN builds the crop from a $128$-dimensional prior through four transposed
convolutions, ran $5$k to $30$k steps and learns its own intensity.

The paper kernel is the three-bandwidth Gaussian MMD of~\cite{recio_2025_train} at
$\sigma = \{0.989, 1.729, 2.236\}$. The protocol we replicate publishes no bandwidths for this
encoding, so we set $\sigma_1$ to make the average Pauli weight $2$, $\sigma_3$ to the square root of
the median heuristic and $\sigma_2$ to their root mean square. The Conv2D-MMD$^2$ bandwidth is
$0.005547$ raw and $0.002558$ flux-aligned. It is fixed once on the held-out features and shared by
every table except the per-class rows. The $20$ floor draws come from the $8{,}710$ validation
images the held-out split leaves. The bandwidth of the intensity estimate, by Silverman's rule of
thumb~\cite{silverman_1986_density}, is $14.64$ at $N{=}11$ and $10.36$ at $N{=}100$.

Applied to the IQP arms, the baselines' validation rule selects $2$k to $16$k steps. Every
Table~\ref{tab:main} cell it changes moves toward better IQP values and no comparison flips, so our
pre-registered $4{,}000$ steps is the conservative choice. At $20{,}000$ fixed steps both baselines
are much worse, so their early stopping matters and we keep it. None of three ablations moves a
generation metric by more than a seed sd. A flat-MMD warm start of $\theta^{0}$, which makes every
stage shot-estimable, leaves the flux-aligned column at $0.0231$ against $0.0225$ at $N{=}11$.
Picking the projection restart by correlation instead of fit loss moves the correlation by
$0.0003$, and two restarts match eight. At $M{=}2$ projecting the mean crop gives $0.765$ and
$0.779$ at the two training-set sizes. Our Conv2D-MMD$^2$ is the kernel inception
distance~\cite{binkowski_2018_demystifying} with an untrained encoder. We do not use the
purpose-built generalisation metrics of~\cite{gili_2024_generalization}, and classical generation
of cosmological images is established~\cite{mustafa_2019_cosmogan}. In ring morphology
(Table~\ref{tab:w1}) the main arm is closest in radius at $N{=}11$ and the DCGAN is closest at
$N{=}100$. Retraining the same DCGAN configuration at the same seed moves its
raw Conv2D-MMD$^2$ by $5$ to $57\%$ and its PDF--JS by $2$ to $74\%$, and in this sense it is the
one arm that is not bit-reproducible.
