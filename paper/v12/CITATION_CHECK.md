## Problems found, and items previously flagged that are now confirmed fixed

### Blocking / substantive

**F1 — `recio_2025_iqpopt` is the wrong citation for the multi-bandwidth kernel (L133 and L471).**
This is the "IQPopt kernel attribution" item from the earlier review, and it is **not yet fixed**. Full text of arXiv:2501.04776 (IQPopt) uses **one** Gaussian bandwidth chosen by the plain median heuristic ("We use the median heuristic [16] to set the bandwidth parameter"; `sigma = median_heuristic(X)`); it contains no three-bandwidth scheme, no mean-Pauli-weight bandwidth rule, and no root-mean-square middle bandwidth. All three components appear, verbatim and together, in **arXiv:2503.02934** (`recio_2025_train`): the loss "ℒ = (1/L) Σᵢ MMD²_{σᵢ}" over "a choice {σ₁,⋯,σ_L} of bandwidths", and "σ₁ is such that the average Pauli weight of Z_a is 2, σ₃ is the square root of the median heuristic, and σ₂ is given by σ₂=√((σ₁²+σ₃²)/2)". The phrase "set by that paper's own rule" at L471 therefore points at the wrong paper.
*Suggested edit (L471–L472):* `the three-bandwidth Gaussian MMD of \cite{recio_2025_train} at $\sigma = \{…\}$, set by that paper's own rule` — optionally `\cite{recio_2025_train}`'s rule as implemented in IQPopt `\cite{recio_2025_iqpopt}` if the software should still be credited.
*Suggested edit (L133):* `the multi-bandwidth Gaussian kernel of~\cite{recio_2025_train}` (or "of the reference framework~\cite{recio_2025_train,recio_2025_iqpopt}").
*Pre-empting the obvious objection:* the IQPopt **package** does ship a `sigma_heuristic(X, n_sigmas)` helper that returns several bandwidths, so "IQPopt" is not absurd as a pointer to multi-bandwidth machinery in general. It does not rescue L471, for two reasons. (i) L471 says "set by **that paper's** own rule", and the cited object is the paper, whose text (6 occurrences of "bandwidth", all singular) states only the median heuristic. (ii) The repo helper interpolates with `np.linspace(sigma_2, med, n_sigmas)`, i.e. an **arithmetic** middle bandwidth, whereas the rule actually used here and stated at L473–L474 is the **root-mean-square** middle from `recio_2025_train`; the two disagree numerically (checked against that paper's Table 1: RMS gives 6.17 ≈ the tabulated 6.1, arithmetic gives 5.85). So neither the IQPopt paper nor the IQPopt code is the source of the rule as described.

No other sentence is affected; `recio_2025_iqpopt` at L79 is correctly cited and should stay.
