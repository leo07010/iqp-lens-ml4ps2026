\begin{abstract}
Instantaneous-quantum-polynomial (IQP) circuits are believed hard to sample
classically~\cite{bremner_2011_classical,bremner_2016_average}, while the expectation values a
maximum-mean-discrepancy (MMD) loss needs stay computable, so such a Born machine trains classically
and deploys on quantum hardware~\cite{recio_2025_train}. A recent correlation--complexity map
predicts which data suit them, and DeepLense strong-lensing images sit at the most IQP-compatible
position we measured, above the turbulence data it was introduced on. The reference protocol's fixed
latent block yields only noise and a learned linear map loses coherence past $M{=}6$, so we let a
small
non-linear map carry the latent. At eleven training images the resulting 10-qubit machine matches a
DCGAN of six times as many parameters on both generation metrics within the seed
spread, leads it on a flux-aligned variant, leads an RBM on all three and reconstructs unseen images
at correlation $0.977$; at $100$ images the DCGAN leads. Every
circuit is simulated exactly at $n=10$ qubits over five seeds, so we claim no quantum advantage,
only a step toward utility where training data are scarce.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

IQP circuits are commuting-gate circuits whose output distribution is the squared Walsh--Hadamard
transform of a phase function. Exact classical sampling from them would collapse the polynomial
hierarchy~\cite{bremner_2011_classical} and the hardness is conjectured to survive approximate
sampling~\cite{bremner_2016_average}, though noise or special structure restores classical
simulability in some cases~\cite{rajakumar_2024_polynomial,codsi_2025_classically}. The same
structure keeps an MMD loss's expectation values computable, so such a model can be \emph{trained
classically and deployed on quantum hardware}~\cite{recio_2025_train,recio_2025_iqpopt}.

Whether real data sit where that is useful is empirical, and a recent
proposal~\cite{liu_2026_correlation} approaches it with two ingredients. (i) A
\emph{correlation--complexity map} placing a dataset at $\qcli$, the Jensen--Shannon divergence
between its Walsh order spectrum and the i.i.d.\ binomial baseline, and $\cci$, how much of its
correlation the best pairwise tree
misses~\cite{watanabe_1960_information,schneidman_2003_network,chow_1968_approximating}, the
high-$\qcli$, high-$\cci$ corner being where IQP circuits should do well. (ii) A latent-adaptation
protocol (Algorithm~1 of that paper, a phase core trained on one anchor plus a fixed block of $50$
Walsh phases adapted sample by sample), both demonstrated on 2D
turbulence~\cite{khojasteh_2022_lagrangian} at $n=18$ qubits.

We take both to simulated dark-matter strong-lensing images from
DeepLense~\cite{alexander_2020_deep,alexander_2021_decoding} and contribute three things. We
recompute the map on lens crops, which land at its most IQP-compatible position, and show with a
permutation null that such a position is necessary and not sufficient. We trace why the reference
latent protocol produces noise on static images and replace its latent map by a non-linear one. We
then compare against an RBM and a DCGAN under two intensity conventions, and report a replication
on the turbulence data in App.~\ref{app:turb}.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Model.}
Every arm is a $10$-qubit IQP Born machine over $1{,}024$ Walsh phases $\theta$, differing only in
how a per-image latent enters. The reference protocol freezes a random block of $50$ of those phases
and adapts it sample by sample, which on static lens crops leaves the other training images poorly
reconstructed (Sec.~\ref{sec:latent}), so we make that map trainable, first as a linear map of $M$
directions,
\begin{equation}
\theta_i \;=\; \theta^{0} + \Phi^{\!\top} z_i,
\qquad \theta^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{1024},\quad
\Phi\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times 1024},\quad z_i \in \mathbb{R}^{M},
\label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
with each row of $\Phi$ normalised in $L^2$. That map degrades once the $\|z\|$ the images demand
leaves the radius over which the Walsh phases interfere coherently, by $M{=}6$ on these crops, so
our main arm replaces $\Phi^{\!\top} z$ by a one-hidden-layer $\tanh$ map $g$ of width $H{=}32$, the
$z_i$ trained jointly with $g$ under a small $L^2$ penalty. The objectives are
the flat MMD (uniform Walsh weights, estimable from hardware shots) and the paper kernel (the
multi-bandwidth Gaussian MMD kernel taken from IQPopt~\cite{recio_2025_iqpopt}), and $\theta^{0}$ is
warm-started by $1{,}000$ exact-KL steps, which a flat-MMD warm
start replaces at no cost (App.~\ref{app:arms}).

\paragraph{Decoding, baselines and metrics.}
A trained pmf is a shape, so it is pasted into a $64\times64$ frame at a total intensity $S$ and a
training-set offset. The reference convention gives every frame the training-set mean $S$, leaving the
generated flux with zero variance where real crops spread by about a quarter of their mean, so we
draw $S$ per image from a kernel density estimate of the $N$ training totals, with no retraining;
App.~\ref{app:fixedS} repeats everything under the fixed $S$. The baselines are a restricted Boltzmann machine
(RBM)~\cite{hinton_2002_training,tieleman_2008_training} and a deep convolutional generative
adversarial network (DCGAN)~\cite{radford_2015_unsupervised}, both early-stopped on $200$ validation
images while the IQP arms run a fixed $4{,}000$ steps, which can only favour the baselines. We score with
two metrics, the Jensen--Shannon divergence between intensity histograms (PDF--JS) and
Conv2D-MMD$^2$ in a fixed random encoder's feature space~\cite{gretton_2012_kernel}, and since the
arms do not agree on how flux is set we add a post-hoc flux-aligned Conv2D-MMD$^2$ rescaling every
frame to the held-out mean total, plus a held-out projection correlation taken over eight restarts
at the lowest fit loss. Bins,
scale and bandwidth are set on the held-out data alone, so no metric moves with the arm scored, and
every arm generates $200$ images against the same $200$ held-out frames at $N \in \{11,100\}$, every
number a mean $\pm$ sample sd over five seeds above a real-vs-real floor.

\paragraph{Departures from the reference setup.}
Five choices differ, each following from the data. Its snapshots interpolate to unseen times,
whereas the DeepLense \texttt{Model~II} images we
use~\cite{alexander_2020_deep,alexander_2023_domain,ml4sci_deeplense} are static, so we generate
unconditionally from the latent prior. It encodes a sample as 18-bit $(x,y,\mathrm{intensity})$
tuples, whereas we centroid-align each image and read its central $32\times32$ crop, $99.4\%$ of the
light, as one probability mass function (pmf) over $1{,}024$ cells at $n=10$ (App.~\ref{app:pipe}),
a raw intensity axis carrying barely one bit. It fixes the latent block, whereas we learn the map
and train the $N$ images jointly, and it estimates its MMD from samples where a $10$-qubit pmf is
exact. It holds out times, so we project onto held-out images instead.

\subsection{From the reference protocol to a non-linear latent map}
\label{sec:latent}

Run sequentially on our low-variance latent block, Algorithm~1 produces noise
(Fig.~\ref{fig:map}(b), second row). Representability is not the cause, since the full
$1{,}024$-phase circuit fits a \emph{single} aligned crop to correlation $0.99997 \pm 0.00001$. The
protocol is, since with the core frozen on one anchor the $50$ fixed coordinates reconstruct every
other training image at only $0.44 \pm 0.04$, fixed Walsh directions cross-fading pixels as an
XOR-shift pattern instead of moving lens structure. Equation~\eqref{eq:affine} repairs that but
introduces a width limit of its own. On a $20$-image
sweep the learned linear map holds a held-out projection correlation of $0.9060 \pm 0.0021$ at
$M{=}2$, falls to $0.7378 \pm 0.0672$ at $M{=}6$ and collapses to $0.2184 \pm 0.1213$ at $M{=}8$.
The non-linear map removes that collapse, holding $0.9368$ to $0.9519$ across the whole sweep, above
the nearest-training-image reference of $0.9339$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:gen}(a)), but not the small useful
width, since on the generation metrics it is flat to $M{=}4$ and degrades from $M{=}8$. We report it
at $M{=}2$, where the held-out projection correlation is $0.9013 \pm 0.0012$ at $N{=}11$ and
$0.9773 \pm 0.0006$ at $N{=}100$.

\subsection{Comparison with classical baselines}
\label{sec:table}

In Table~\ref{tab:main} the eleven-image regime is where the IQP arms are competitive. There the
non-linear arm matches the DCGAN on PDF--JS and raw Conv2D-MMD$^2$ within seed noise ($-0.13$ and
$-0.04$ of the DCGAN's own sd) and leads on the flux-aligned column ($+1.57$ sd), and it leads the
RBM on all three by $0.75$, $1.40$ and $2.27$ of the RBM's sd, with six times fewer parameters. Under
the paper kernel the same map holds the table's best mean PDF--JS, $0.75 \times 10^{-3}$ against the
DCGAN's $1.11 \pm 0.56$, which leaves the two inside one sd, and trades that for a worse
flux-aligned column. No arm beats the DCGAN on all three columns by more
than a seed sd at either size, and at $100$ images the DCGAN leads on PDF--JS and raw
Conv2D-MMD$^2$, the flux-aligned $1.8 \pm 0.2$ sitting inside one sd of its $2.1 \pm 0.4$.

Two properties bound how the table reads. The flux-aligned column is invariant to the intensity
convention rather than independent of the raw one, the largest paired difference there being
$6\times10^{-9}$ over our $325$ runs, so the two MMD$^2$ columns measure shape, and shape plus flux,
not two separate things. The sampling rule is also not neutral between arms, improving every IQP
arm's raw Conv2D-MMD$^2$ by $22$ to $39\%$ at $N{=}11$ while making the RBM's $20\%$ worse and
improving it by $13\%$ at $N{=}100$, so part of the eleven-image gap comes from the baseline moving.
Sampling fixes the shape of the generated flux distribution and not its location, the eleven
training frames being $9\%$ dimmer than the held-out set, which is why the raw column stops beside
the DCGAN.

\paragraph{Data scaling and per-class training.}
\label{sec:scaling}
Across $N \in \{10,\dots,100\}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:scaling}) the non-linear arm's raw Conv2D-MMD$^2$
falls from $0.062 \pm 0.016$ to $0.024 \pm 0.005$ and the RBM's from $0.091 \pm 0.017$ to
$0.051 \pm 0.008$, the DCGAN starting ahead at $N{=}10$ ($0.046 \pm 0.017$) and ending level. On the flux-aligned column the non-linear arm holds the lowest mean at every $N$,
though only at $N{=}10$ and $N{=}70$ by more than a seed sd. Retrained inside one class at $N{=}100$
it reaches flux-aligned $0.017$, $0.015$ and $0.021$ for axion, cdm and no-sub against floors of
$0.0031$, $0.0032$ and $0.0045$ (App.~\ref{app:sweeps}), so each class can be generated on its own,
not that the model separates them.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:disc}

At eleven training images a $10$-qubit IQP Born machine with a learned non-linear latent map is
competitive with an RBM and a DCGAN and on the flux-aligned metric surpasses both
at a sixth of the DCGAN's parameters, a step toward utility where training data is the scarce
resource. Nothing here is evidence of quantum advantage, since every circuit is simulated exactly at
$n=10$ qubits and the hardness results describe the class at scale, and the standing does not
survive to $100$ images. Five seeds are few, the DCGAN adds a spread of up to $57\%$ on raw
Conv2D-MMD$^2$ at one seed, and the sampled intensity moves the raw column unequally across arms.

\section{Replication of the reference paper's turbulence experiment}
\label{app:turb}

On the reference paper's own dataset (2D cylinder-wake turbulence D2~\cite{khojasteh_2022_lagrangian},
$n=18$ qubits, 11 anchor snapshots, five seeds) the result is \textbf{not reproduced} under our
pre-registered convention, the RBM (PDF--JS $0.217 \pm 0.007$) and a 1.2M-parameter DCGAN
($0.045 \pm 0.062$) beating our 31k-parameter IQP ($0.272 \pm 0.004$) on \emph{both} metrics. Under
a last-write-wins aggregation convention, never specified in the paper,
our PDF--JS values land close to the published ones ($0.0324$ and $0.0473$ against $0.0238$ and
$0.0221$), but the RBM still wins; and Conv2D-MMD$^2$ is saturated, ground truth shuffled in pixel
space already scoring $0.7045 \pm 0.0095$, below every model in that paper's Table~2.

\section{Training and evaluation details}
\label{app:arms}

\paragraph{Table conventions.} The $^{\dagger}$ arm runs Algorithm~1 on our low-variance latent
block, whose stage~1 trains the phase core and the anchor's latent block jointly where the reference
protocol freezes a random one. Params count the shared core plus the per-image latent
block at each $N$, the DCGAN entry is its generator alone, and the RBM holds $10{,}960$ shared
weights plus $50$ per image; it ran $160$k or $80$k steps and the DCGAN $5$k to $30$k.

\paragraph{Protocol details.} The paper kernel is the three-bandwidth Gaussian MMD of
IQPopt~\cite{recio_2025_iqpopt} at $\sigma = \{0.989, 1.729, 2.236\}$, that paper publishing none.
The Conv2D-MMD$^2$ bandwidth, fixed once on the held-out
features and shared by every table, is $0.005547$ raw and $0.002558$ flux-aligned, the $20$ floor
draws come from the $8{,}710$ validation images the held-out split leaves, and the Silverman
bandwidth of the intensity estimate is $14.64$ at $N{=}11$ and $10.36$ at $N{=}100$. Applying the
baselines' validation rule to the IQP arms selects $1$k to $16$k steps and changes no cell of
Table~\ref{tab:main} by more than one sd, so we keep the fixed $4{,}000$ steps; at $20{,}000$ fixed
steps both baselines are much worse, so their early stopping is load-bearing and is granted
throughout. The DCGAN is the only arm here that is not bit-reproducible.

\paragraph{Ablations.} Three settings of the main arm were varied and none moves a metric by more
than a seed sd. A flat-MMD warm start of $\theta^{0}$ in place of the exact-KL one, making every
stage shot-estimable, leaves flux-aligned Conv2D-MMD$^2$ at $0.0231$ against $0.0225$ at $N{=}11$;
picking the projection restart by correlation rather than by fit loss moves the correlation by
$0.0003$; and two restarts match eight, though the zero-initialised restart alone reaches only
$0.838$ and $0.887$ against $0.901$ and $0.977$.
