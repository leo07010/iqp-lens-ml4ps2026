\begin{abstract}
Instantaneous-quantum-polynomial (IQP) circuits are believed hard to sample
classically~\cite{bremner_2011_classical,bremner_2016_average}, while the expectation values a
maximum-mean-discrepancy (MMD) loss needs stay computable, so such a Born machine can be trained
classically and deployed on quantum hardware~\cite{recio_2025_train}. A recent
correlation--complexity map is proposed to indicate which data suit them, and DeepLense
strong-lensing images sit at the most IQP-compatible position among the encodings we measured. The
reference protocol's frozen core with sequential
adaptation yields only noise, which joint training repairs, and a learned linear map then loses
coherence past $M{=}6$, so we let a small non-linear map carry the latent. At eleven training images
the resulting 10-qubit machine matches a DCGAN of six times as many parameters on both generation
metrics within the seed spread, is ahead of it on a flux-aligned variant and of an RBM on the two
MMD$^2$ columns while level with it on the histogram metric, and projects unseen images at
correlation $0.901$; at $100$ images the DCGAN is ahead and that correlation reaches $0.977$. Every
circuit is simulated exactly at $n=10$ qubits over five seeds, so we claim no quantum advantage, only
an early test of whether such a model could be worth deploying where training data are scarce.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

IQP circuits are commuting-gate circuits whose output distribution is the squared
Walsh--Hadamard~\cite{walsh_1923_closed} transform of a phase function. Exact or multiplicatively
approximate classical sampling from them would collapse the polynomial
hierarchy~\cite{bremner_2011_classical}, and that hardness is conjectured to hold on average under
additive error~\cite{bremner_2016_average}, a bounded motivation, since noise makes such circuits
simulable in polynomial time~\cite{rajakumar_2024_polynomial}, classical algorithms keep
improving~\cite{maslov_2024_fast} and trainability itself pulls towards
simulability~\cite{cerezo_2025_does}. The same structure keeps an MMD loss's expectation values
computable~\cite{recio_2025_iqpopt}, as it does for other encodings built to stay
simulable~\cite{lizziobosco_2026_pauli}, so such a model can be \emph{trained
classically and deployed on quantum hardware}~\cite{recio_2025_train}, in the line of Born machines
trained by an MMD loss~\cite{liu_2018_differentiable,benedetti_2019_generative,coyle_2020_born}.

Whether real data sit where that is useful is empirical, and a recent
proposal~\cite{liu_2026_correlation} approaches it with two ingredients. (i) A
\emph{correlation--complexity map} placing a dataset at $\qcli$, the Jensen--Shannon divergence
between its Walsh order spectrum, the Fourier power at each Hamming weight, and the i.i.d.\ binomial
baseline, and $\cci \in [0,1]$, the share of its correlation the best pairwise tree
misses~\cite{watanabe_1960_information,schneidman_2003_network,chow_1968_approximating}, the
high-$\qcli$, high-$\cci$ corner being where IQP circuits should do well. (ii) A latent-adaptation
protocol (Algorithm~1 of that paper's v2, a phase core trained on one anchor plus the first $50$
phases in Hamming order, randomly initialised and adapted sample by sample), demonstrated at $n=18$
qubits on 2D turbulence~\cite{khojasteh_2022_lagrangian}, one of several datasets the map covers. A follow-up
initialises that same fixed block from a physics-informed surrogate~\cite{liu_2026_physics}, where we
instead learn the directions.

We take both to simulated dark-matter strong-lensing images from
DeepLense~\cite{alexander_2020_deep,alexander_2021_decoding} and contribute three things. We
recompute the map on lens crops, which land at the most IQP-compatible position we measured, and
show with a permutation null that there the index is largely determined by the intensity histogram,
so a favourable position is not by itself evidence of IQP-suitable structure. We trace why the
reference latent protocol produces noise on static images and replace its latent map by a non-linear
one. We then compare against an RBM and a DCGAN under two intensity conventions, and report a
replication on the turbulence data in App.~\ref{app:turb}.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Model.}
Every arm is a $10$-qubit IQP Born machine over $1{,}024$ Walsh phases $\theta$, one per qubit
subset and $1{,}023$ of them effective since the empty subset is a global phase, differing only in
how a per-image latent enters. The reference protocol freezes the first $50$ of
those phases in Hamming order and adapts them sample by sample, which on static crops leaves the
other training images poorly reconstructed (Sec.~\ref{sec:latent}), so we make that map trainable,
first as a linear map of $M$ directions,
\begin{equation}
\theta_i \;=\; \theta^{0} + \Phi^{\!\top} z_i,
\qquad \theta^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{1024},\quad
\Phi\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times 1024},\quad z_i \in \mathbb{R}^{M},
\label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
with each row of $\Phi$ normalised in $L^2$. That map degrades by $M{=}6$ on these crops, we suspect
once the $\|z\|$ the images demand leaves the radius over which the Walsh phases interfere
coherently, though we do not isolate the cause; our main arm replaces $\Phi^{\!\top} z$ by a
one-hidden-layer $\tanh$ map $g$ of width $H{=}32$, the $z_i$ trained jointly with $g$ under a small
$L^2$ penalty. The main objective is the flat MMD (uniform Walsh weights, estimable from hardware
shots), with the three-bandwidth Gaussian kernel of~\cite{recio_2025_train}, the \emph{paper
kernel} of the tables, kept as a secondary arm, and $\theta^{0}$ is warm-started by
$1{,}000$ exact-KL steps, which a flat-MMD warm start replaces at no cost (App.~\ref{app:arms}).

\paragraph{Decoding, baselines and metrics.}
The trained pmf fixes only the relative intensities, so it is pasted into a $64\times64$ frame at a
total intensity $S$ and at an offset drawn from the training crops' own offsets. The reference
convention gives every frame the training-set mean $S$, leaving the generated flux with zero variance
where real crops spread by about a quarter of their mean, so we draw $S$ per image from a kernel
density estimate of the $N$ training totals, with no retraining;
App.~\ref{app:fixedS} repeats everything under the fixed $S$. The baselines are a restricted Boltzmann machine
(RBM)~\cite{smolensky_1986_information,hinton_2002_training,tieleman_2008_training} and a deep convolutional generative
adversarial network (DCGAN)~\cite{radford_2015_unsupervised}, both early-stopped on $200$ validation
images while the IQP arms run a fixed $4{,}000$ steps, which can only favour them. We score with the
Jensen--Shannon divergence between intensity histograms (PDF--JS) and Conv2D-MMD$^2$ in a fixed
random encoder's feature space~\cite{gretton_2012_kernel,binkowski_2018_demystifying}, add a
post-hoc flux-aligned
Conv2D-MMD$^2$ rescaling every frame to the held-out mean total since the arms do not agree on how
flux is set, and report a held-out projection correlation, the best of eight latent restarts by the
fit's own loss. Bins, scale and bandwidth are set on the held-out data alone, so no metric moves with the arm scored, and every arm generates $200$ images against the
same $200$ held-out frames at $N \in \{11,100\}$, every number a mean $\pm$ sample sd over five seeds
beside a real-vs-real floor between two disjoint real sets. The $N$
training images are fixed across seeds, so that spread is initialisation and sampling variance alone.
A lead in sd means the difference of two means in units of the baseline's seed sd, descriptive and
not a significance test.

\paragraph{Departures from the reference setup.}
Four choices differ, each following from the data. The reference snapshots interpolate to unseen
times, whereas the DeepLense \texttt{Model~II} images we use~\cite{ml4sci_deeplense} are static, so we
generate unconditionally and project onto held-out images. Its 18-bit $(x,y,\mathrm{intensity})$
encoding suits a field of tuples, whereas a lens crop's raw intensity axis carries barely one bit, so
we centroid-align each image and read its central $32\times32$ crop, $99.4\%$ of the light, as one
probability mass function (pmf) over $1{,}024$ cells at $n=10$ (App.~\ref{app:pipe}). It fixes the
latent block, whereas we learn the map and train the $N$ images jointly, and it estimates its MMD
from samples where a $10$-qubit pmf is exact.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Where lens crops sit on the correlation--complexity map}
\label{sec:map}

Table~\ref{tab:map} recomputes the map (Fig.~\ref{fig:map}(a)). Encoded as the reference paper
encodes turbulence, lens images sit far above it in $\qcli$ and inside the corner the map calls
IQP-compatible, yet a permutation null keeping each image's intensity histogram comes within $14\%$
of the lens value and a factor of two of the much smaller turbulence value, so there the index
largely tracks the histogram. Both indices depend on $K$, the codes per image, so a row reads only
against its own-$K$ null; at the protocol's own $N_t = 5\times10^{4}$ samples per
snapshot~\cite{liu_2026_correlation} the turbulence $\qcli$ falls to $0.091$ against a null of
$0.098$. The 10-bit crop pmf, the encoding we model, is the one that separates from its null in both
coordinates, but sits at $\cci = 0.43$, outside the high-$\cci$ corner, so its favourable $\qcli$
motivates the encoding, not the choice of an IQP over a pairwise model.

\subsection{From the reference protocol to a non-linear latent map}
\label{sec:latent}

Run sequentially on our own $50$-phase block (App.~\ref{app:arms}), Algorithm~1 produces noise
(Fig.~\ref{fig:map}(b), second row). Neither representability nor the block is the cause, since the
full circuit fits a \emph{single} aligned crop to correlation $0.99997 \pm 0.00001$ and training the
same $50$ phases jointly with the core across all $N$ images (the \emph{fixed basis, joint} row of
Table~\ref{tab:main}) already recovers most of the gap. What fails is the frozen core
with sequential per-image adaptation, since with the core frozen on one anchor the $50$ fixed
coordinates reconstruct every other training image at only $0.44 \pm 0.04$; the block size of $50$
is not swept. Equation~\eqref{eq:affine} repairs the rest but introduces a width limit of its
own. On a $20$-image sweep the learned linear map holds a held-out projection correlation of
$0.906 \pm 0.002$ at $M{=}2$, falls to $0.738 \pm 0.067$ at $M{=}6$ and collapses to
$0.218 \pm 0.121$ at $M{=}8$. The non-linear map removes that collapse, holding $0.9368$ to
$0.9519$ across the sweep, a little above that split's nearest-training-image reference of $0.9339$;
the two sweeps come from separate drivers differing in optimiser schedule
and initialisation, both
scored on the same $20$ held-out frames, so a small offset between them is not a model difference. It does not
buy a large useful width, being flat to $M{=}4$ and degrading from $M{=}8$. We report it at $M{=}2$, where the held-out projection correlation is $0.901$ at $N{=}11$
and $0.977$ at $N{=}100$, against $0.892$ and $0.970$ for projecting the nearest training image and
$0.838$ and $0.887$ for a single zero-initialised restart.

In Table~\ref{tab:main} the eleven-image regime is where the IQP arms are competitive. There the
non-linear arm matches the DCGAN on PDF--JS and raw Conv2D-MMD$^2$ within seed noise ($-0.13$ and
$-0.04$ of the DCGAN's own sd) and is ahead on the flux-aligned column ($+1.57$ sd), and against the
RBM it is ahead on the two MMD$^2$ columns by $1.40$ and $2.27$ of the RBM's sd with PDF--JS inside
one sd ($0.75$), at a sixth of the DCGAN's parameters and three times the RBM's. Under the paper
kernel the same map holds the best mean PDF--JS, $0.75 \times 10^{-3}$ against the DCGAN's
$1.1 \pm 0.6$, which leaves the two inside one sd, and trades that for a worse flux-aligned column.
No arm is ahead of the DCGAN on all three columns by more than a seed sd at either size, and at $100$
images it is ahead on PDF--JS and raw Conv2D-MMD$^2$, the flux-aligned $1.8 \pm 0.2$ sitting inside
one sd of its $2.1 \pm 0.4$.

Three properties bound how the table reads. The flux-aligned column is invariant to the intensity
convention, the largest paired difference being $6\times10^{-9}$ over the $325$ runs carrying both.
That is invariance, not independence from the raw column, so the two MMD$^2$ columns measure shape,
and shape plus flux, not two separate things. The sampling rule is also not
neutral between arms, improving every IQP arm's raw Conv2D-MMD$^2$ by $22$ to $39\%$ at $N{=}11$
while making the RBM's $20\%$ worse, and under the fixed intensity the RBM leads the non-linear arm
there ($6.5 \pm 0.8$ against $8.5 \pm 0.2$, App.~\ref{app:fixedS}), so the raw-column
standing against the RBM is a property of the sampled decode and not of the model. Sampling fixes the
shape of the generated flux distribution and not its location, the eleven training frames being $9\%$
dimmer than the held-out set, which is why the raw column stops there.

Data scaling over $N \in \{10,\dots,100\}$ and per-class retraining are reported in
App.~\ref{app:sweeps}.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:disc}

At eleven training images a $10$-qubit IQP Born machine with a learned non-linear latent map is
competitive with an RBM and a DCGAN, matching the DCGAN on both generation metrics within the seed
spread at a sixth of its parameters and holding the lowest flux-aligned mean; that standing does not
survive to $100$ images. Nothing here is evidence of quantum advantage, since every circuit is
simulated exactly at $n=10$ qubits, and any deployed margin is bounded by connectivity and
noise~\cite{placidi_2026_connectivity}. Generated frames sit farther in $L_2$ from their
nearest training image than real held-out frames do ($4.21 \pm 0.10$ against $2.90$ at $N{=}11$), so
the arm is not memorising. Two steps are not shot-estimable as run, the exact-KL
warm start, replaced in App.~\ref{app:arms} within one seed sd, and the projection, which
optimises $z$ against the exact pmf; and at $n=10$ we parameterise all $2^{n}$ Walsh phases where the
scaling up would need them sparsified~\cite{liu_2026_correlation}, untested here. Only the model fit
sees $N$ images, while bandwidths, floors and the baselines' early stopping use hundreds to thousands
of real frames, so this is a small-training-set study, not a small-data pipeline; alignment and the
crop leave translation and the outer $0.6\%$ of the light outside the task, floor included. Five
seeds are few, the DCGAN adds up to $57\%$ of spread at one seed and alone is not bit-reproducible,
the sampled intensity moves the raw column unequally, everything rests on one simulated dataset, the
metrics do not test substructure, and no destroyed image is scored as an upper reference.

\label{app:sweeps}

Hidden width matters less than latent width, everything from $H{=}16$ up being inside a seed sd, and
the latent-width sweep is flat over $M \le 4$ before degrading at $M{=}8$ and collapsing at $M{=}16$;
both full sweeps are in the released seed tables. Across $N \in \{10,30,50,70,90,100\}$ the non-linear arm's raw
Conv2D-MMD$^2$ falls from $0.062 \pm 0.016$ to $0.024 \pm 0.005$ and the RBM's from

\section{Turbulence replication, training and evaluation details}
\label{app:turb}

On that paper's own dataset (2D cylinder-wake turbulence D2~\cite{khojasteh_2022_lagrangian},
$n=18$ qubits, 11 anchor snapshots, five seeds) the result is \textbf{not reproduced} under our
pre-registered convention, the RBM (PDF--JS $0.217 \pm 0.007$) and a 1.2M-parameter DCGAN
($0.045 \pm 0.062$) both scoring better than our 31k-parameter IQP ($0.272 \pm 0.004$) on
\emph{both} metrics. Under a last-write-wins aggregation, never specified in that
paper~\cite{liu_2026_correlation}, our PDF--JS moves towards the published values without
reaching them ($0.0324$, $0.0473$ against $0.0238$, $0.0221$), and the RBM still leads.
Conv2D-MMD$^2$ there does not rank a destroyed image below a trained one, pixel-shuffled ground truth
scoring $0.7045 \pm 0.0095$, better than every model in that paper's Table~2; that paper notes itself
that its baselines are not exhaustive and that this metric is no substitute for spectral
diagnostics~\cite{liu_2026_correlation}.

\label{app:arms}

\paragraph{Arms, baselines and protocol.} The $^{\dagger}$ arm runs Algorithm~1 of
v2~\cite{liu_2026_correlation} on our own latent block, the $50$ subsets of Hamming weight at most
$2$ whose Walsh coefficients vary most over a disjoint pool of $2{,}000$ images, where the reference
block is the first $50$ in Hamming order; \emph{fixed basis, joint} is that block trained jointly
with the core across all $N$ images. Params are totals at that $N$, shared ($\theta^{0}$ included)
plus $N$ per-image latents, so the fixed-basis arms hold $974 + 50N$, the learned-direction arms
$5{,}120 + MN$ and the RBM $10{,}960 + 50N$; the DCGAN entry is its generator alone, its
discriminator adding $53{,}497$. The RBM has $10$ visible and $1{,}000$ hidden units on the $10$-bit
code, each image owning its visible biases and the first $40$ hidden biases, and ran $160$k or $80$k
steps; the DCGAN builds the crop from a $128$-dimensional prior through four transposed
convolutions, ran $5$k to $30$k steps and learns its own intensity. The paper kernel is the three-bandwidth Gaussian MMD
of~\cite{recio_2025_train} at $\sigma = \{0.989, 1.729, 2.236\}$, with $\sigma_1$ set so the average
Pauli weight is $2$, $\sigma_3$ the square root of the median heuristic and $\sigma_2$ their root
mean square, since the protocol we replicate publishes none for this encoding. The Conv2D-MMD$^2$
bandwidth is $0.005547$ raw and $0.002558$ flux-aligned, fixed once on the held-out features and
shared by every table except the per-class rows; the $20$ floor draws come from the $8{,}710$
validation images the held-out split leaves; and the Silverman
bandwidth~\cite{silverman_1986_density} of the intensity estimate is $14.64$ at $N{=}11$ and $10.36$
at $N{=}100$. The baselines' validation rule applied to the IQP arms selects $2$k to $16$k steps and
moves every Table~\ref{tab:main} cell it changes toward better IQP values without altering a
comparison, so our pre-registered $4{,}000$ steps is the conservative choice, and at $20{,}000$ fixed
steps both baselines are much worse, so their early stopping is load-bearing and granted. Of
three ablations none moves a generation metric by more than a seed sd.
A flat-MMD warm start of $\theta^{0}$, making every stage shot-estimable, leaves the flux-aligned
column at $0.0231$ against $0.0225$ at $N{=}11$; picking the projection restart by correlation rather
than by fit loss moves the correlation by $0.0003$; and two restarts match eight ($0.901$, $0.977$)
where one zero-initialised restart reaches $0.838$ and $0.887$. The projection references at $M{=}2$
are $0.892$ and $0.970$ for the nearest training image and $0.765$ and $0.779$ for the mean crop. Our Conv2D-MMD$^2$ is
the kernel inception distance~\cite{binkowski_2018_demystifying} with an untrained encoder, we do not
use the purpose-built generalisation metrics of~\cite{gili_2024_generalization}, and classical
generation of cosmological images is established~\cite{mustafa_2019_cosmogan}. As Wasserstein-1
distance in ring radius and the two ellipticity components the main arm is at
$0.82 / 0.33 / 0.35$ at $N{=}11$ against $3.23 / 0.46 / 0.24$ (RBM), $1.67 / 0.62 / 0.40$ (DCGAN) and
$0.10 / 0.17 / 0.10$ between two real sets; at $N{=}100$ the DCGAN is closest, $0.40 / 0.28 / 0.15$
against $1.01 / 0.31 / 0.14$. Retraining the same DCGAN configuration at the same seed moves its raw
Conv2D-MMD$^2$ by $5$ to $57\%$ and its PDF--JS by $2$ to $74\%$, which is what we mean by the one
arm that is not bit-reproducible.
