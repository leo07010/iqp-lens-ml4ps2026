# Seed tables — part B (map, M sweep, App. C, App. D)

Every number below is a **mean ± sd over 5 seeds**, sample sd (`ddof=1`). Nothing here is
typed by hand: this file is generated by `make_seed_tables_b.py` from three json
aggregates, each written by a SLURM job. `main.tex` is **not** modified by this work.

A "seed" means something different in each block, and the difference matters more than the
numbers do:

| block | what the seed moves | what it does **not** move |
|---|---|---|
| Table 1 (map) | the K-sample draws, the permutation nulls, the i.i.d.-uniform nulls | the data split. `data_lens_r3` / `data_lens` pin their splits at their own `SPLIT_SEED` and explicitly decouple them from any arm seed, so the same 200 held-out images are used in every seed **by design** |
| Fig. 2(a) (M sweep) | the model only: θ⁰'s random start, Φ's draw, z's draw | the 20 training and 20 held-out images |
| App. C (Fig. 3) | the learner's initial angles | the 140 target circuits, their 10⁴-sample datasets, their kernel bandwidths, and the three ≤2-local learner architectures |
| App. D (Fig. 4) | one whole SPSA trajectory: mask set, initial angles, perturbation stream | the system sizes, gain settings and step count |

## 1. Table 1 — correlation–complexity map (5 seeds)

`run_map.py --seed 0..4 --with-d2`, unchanged, then `agg_map_seeds.py`.

**"K = 4,096" means two different things on the two 18-bit rows**, which is why the third
column is here. A 64×64 lens image contributes each of the 4,096 (k_x, k_y) cells exactly
once, carrying that cell's own k_z: the pool is an **exhaustive sweep**, not a sample, so
that row has no sampling noise at all (sd 0.0000, and that is exact, not rounding). The
turbulence row at the same K is a random **subsample** of the snapshot's native grid, taken
to match the lens K, and it does carry sampling noise. The turbulence row at the reference
paper's own N_t = 5×10⁴ is included so the comparison can also be read at the paper's
sample count.

| Dataset and encoding | K | how K is obtained | I_QCLI | I_CCI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lens, 18-bit (x,y,z) tuples | 4096 | exhaustive: one k_z per (k_x,k_y) cell of the 64x64 image | 0.4715 ± 0.0000 | 0.8677 ± 0.0000 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;permutation null | 4096 | (same K) | 0.4154 ± 0.0000 | 0.8783 ± 0.0001 |
| Turbulence D2, 18-bit tuples | 4096 | subsampled from the native grid to match the lens K | 0.0232 ± 0.0002 | 0.9285 ± 0.0008 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;permutation null | 4096 | (same K) | 0.0114 ± 0.0000 | 0.9371 ± 0.0009 |
| Turbulence D2, 18-bit tuples (native K) | 50000 | the reference paper's own N_t = 5x10^4 draws per snapshot | 0.0910 ± 0.0000 | 0.9085 ± 0.0000 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;permutation null | 50000 | (same K) | 0.0981 ± 0.0007 | 0.8782 ± 0.0003 |
| Lens, 10-bit crop pmf | exact pmf | no sampling: the exact population pmf of the 32x32 crop | 0.4731 ± 0.0000 | 0.4313 ± 0.0000 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;permutation null | exact pmf | (same K) | 0.1782 ± 0.0000 | 0.9634 ± 0.0004 |

**Reproduction check.** Re-running seed 0 reproduces the committed `results_map.json`
with max |diff| = **0.0e+00** across all quoted fields.

**Reading.** No seed spread exceeds 0.0009, i.e. every entry is stable at the fourth decimal,
far inside the 3 % noise band the project uses for calling a difference real. Table 1's
conclusions are unchanged: the lens 18-bit row sits far above turbulence in I_QCLI but is
nearly matched by its own permutation null, and only the 10-bit crop pmf separates from its
null in both coordinates.

**One thing the new native-K row adds.** At K = 5×10⁴ the turbulence I_QCLI (0.0910) is
*below* its own permutation null (0.0981), whereas at the subsampled K = 4,096 it is above
it. Both indices are strongly K-dependent finite-sample statistics, so a turbulence row
can only be read against a null at its own K — which is exactly why the table carries
one per row rather than a single global null.

## 2. Fig. 2(a) — held-out projection correlation vs M (5 model seeds)

`mech_seeds.py --seed 0..4` + `agg_mech_seeds.py`. One process reproduces all three of the
held-out projection protocols the seed-0 sweep was spread over, each applied to exactly the
M values it produced at seed 0.

| M | held-out projection corr. | train corr. | protocol (seed-0 source) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.7439 ± 0.0000 | 0.7861 ± 0.0000 | `results.json` |
| 2 | 0.9060 ± 0.0021 | 0.9456 ± 0.0009 | `diag3.json` |
| 4 | 0.8577 ± 0.0559 | 0.9325 ± 0.0217 | `results.json` |
| 6 | 0.7378 ± 0.0672 | 0.9237 ± 0.0201 | `diag3.json` |
| 8 | 0.2184 ± 0.1213 | 0.9486 ± 0.0009 | `diag.json` (lr × init sweep, best) |
| 16 | 0.1457 ± 0.0048 | 0.9965 ± 0.0002 | `diag.json` (lr × init sweep, best) |

| reference level | value | note |
|---|---|---|
| nearest training image | 0.9339 ± 0.0000 | function of the fixed split alone — sd is exactly 0 |
| mean training image | 0.7439 ± 0.0000 | same |
| random direction (**paired**, drawn in the figure) | 0.7458 ± 0.0005 | untrained Φ on the **M = 0 θ⁰**, the offset that was itself fitted without any latent |
| random direction (mismatched, used at seed 0) | 0.6468 ± 0.0484 | untrained Φ on the **trained M = 4 θ⁰**, which was optimised jointly with a *trained* Φ |

The paired control is the right one and it is the harder bar: an untrained direction on a
latent-free offset already reaches 0.746, so a learned latent has to beat that, not the
0.647 of the mismatched pairing.

**Reproduction check.** Seed 0 reproduces all 11 published items — the six M-sweep points,
the two data-only reference levels and the diag2 random-direction draws — with max |diff| =
**0.0e+00**.

**Thread count is part of the protocol.** `affine_latent.py` sets
`torch.set_num_threads(4)`. At 2 threads the joint Adam fit for M ≥ 6 lands on a slightly
different optimum (the reduction order of the `z @ Φ` matmul changes) and seed 0 then misses
the published M = 6/8/16 values by ~7e-3 while M = 0/2/4 still match to 1e-10. The reported
run is at 4 threads and reproduces all six exactly; the 2-thread run is kept under
`mech_tests/mech_test2/threads2_run/` as the record.

**This is the block where seeds change a conclusion.** At seed 0 the sweep peaks at M = 4
(0.9113). Over five seeds M = 4 is 0.8577 ± 0.0559 while M = 2 is 0.9060 ± 0.0021 — M = 2 is both higher on
average and an order of magnitude more stable (sd 0.0021 vs 0.0559), and seed 0 is the **best**
of the five M = 4 runs (per-seed: 0.9113, 0.8979, 0.8839, 0.8066, 0.7889).
The collapse at M = 8, 16 survives: both stay far below every reference level in every seed.
What does not survive as stated is the claim that M = 4 is the working point; the honest
version is that M = 2 and M = 4 are the working region, with M = 2 the more reliable of the
two. M = 6 (0.7378 ± 0.0672) now sits at the mean-image level (0.7439) rather than above it.

M = 0 is init-independent (0.7439 ± 0.0000, per-seed spread 8.9e-05) even though its θ⁰ start does vary with
the seed — the latent-free fit converges to the same solution from every start.

## 3. App. C (reference paper Fig. 3) — 5 learner seeds, targets fixed

`fig3_learner_seeds.py --learner-seeds 0,1,2,3,4` + `agg_fig34_seeds.py`. The claim App. C
supports is about the **learner**, so the learner's seed is the one repeated; `--seed` stays
at the published 20260907, which is what keeps the targets and the learner architectures
fixed.

| G_n | median MMD² | Spearman(MMD², target I_QCLI) | median MMD² (exact-pmf-trained control) | Spearman (control) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 9.578e-03 ± 1.1e-05 | +0.9786 ± 0.0010 | 9.453e-03 ± 1.9e-05 | +0.9812 ± 0.0012 |
| 100 | 2.147e-03 ± 6.8e-06 | +0.9009 ± 0.0014 | 2.109e-03 ± 1.3e-05 | +0.9019 ± 0.0016 |
| 150 | 1.437e-03 ± 2.0e-05 | +0.8091 ± 0.0039 | 1.374e-03 ± 1.4e-05 | +0.8057 ± 0.0048 |

| G_n | Spearman(floor proxy, I_QCLI), 10⁴-sample target | same, exact-pmf target |
|---|---|---|
| 50 | -0.9841 ± 0.0000 | -0.9876 ± 0.0000 |
| 100 | -0.9842 ± 0.0001 | -0.9883 ± 0.0000 |
| 150 | -0.9291 ± 0.0000 | -0.9956 ± 0.0000 |

Both floor proxies are functions of the data spectrum and the learner's **fixed** Pauli
support only, so they cannot move with the learner's initial angles: their sd over learner
seeds is 0 **by construction**, not by luck, and the table says so rather than presenting
it as a stability result.

**One wording issue this surfaces.** App. C currently says "ρ ≤ −0.98 for the
Proposition II.2 floor proxy". That holds for all three learners only for the **exact-pmf**
proxy (worst case -0.9876). For the 10⁴-sample proxy the G_n = 150 learner is -0.9291, which is
not ≤ −0.98. The sentence needs to name which proxy it means (or quote -0.93 as the bound).

**Reproduction check.** Learner seed 0 re-derives `results_fig3_v2.jsonl` with max |diff| =
**1.26e-05** over 1260 values. That is float64 GPU non-determinism between compute nodes, not a
protocol difference: every statistic App. C quotes agrees to 5 significant figures
(medians 9.5718e-3 / 2.1527e-3 / 1.4500e-3, Spearman +0.9795 / +0.9014 / +0.8087).

**Reading.** App. C is the most seed-robust block of the four: no Spearman moves by more
than 0.004 and no median by more than 2.0e-05 across the five learner seeds. Both published
claims stand: the envelope falls with learner gate count, and inside the reachable range the
achieved MMD² **rises** with the target's QCLI, the opposite of the reference paper's
claim (a).

## 4. App. D (reference paper Fig. 4) — 5 SPSA seeds

`results_fig4.jsonl` (seeds 0–2, published) + `results_fig4_seeds34.jsonl` (seeds 3–4).

**Two statistics, not one.** App. C's numbers are computed over 140 targets inside one
seed, so a per-seed mean ± sd is the natural summary. App. D's published ρ values were
computed on the three seeds **pooled into one cloud**, and pooling trajectories with
different QCLI offsets is not the same statistic as averaging their individual ρ. Both are
given; the paper's current sentences refer to the pooled column.

| n | global ρ, per-seed mean ± sd | global ρ, pooled (5 seeds) | ρ for I_QCLI ≤ 0.3, per-seed | ρ for I_QCLI ≤ 0.3, pooled | exact uniform calib. I_QCLI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | +0.2534 ± 0.9210 | +0.0814 | +0.9159 ± 0.0987 | +0.7923 | 0.9816 |
| 12 | -0.5240 ± 0.3621 | -0.5673 | +0.9051 ± 0.0440 | +0.7938 | 0.9984 |
| 16 | -0.6338 ± 0.0985 | -0.6352 | +0.9554 ± 0.0105 | +0.9001 | 0.9999 |

Per-seed global ρ, which is where the instability lives:

* n = 8: s0 +0.994  s1 +0.959  s2 +0.808  s3 -0.889  s4 -0.604
* n = 12: s0 -0.815  s1 +0.077  s2 -0.775  s3 -0.471  s4 -0.636
* n = 16: s0 -0.674  s1 -0.480  s2 -0.596  s3 -0.727  s4 -0.692

**This block changes a number in the paper.** App. D quotes ρ = +0.83 / +0.76 / +0.95 for
I_QCLI ≤ 0.3 and global ρ = −0.53, −0.57 at n = 12, 16, all from 3 pooled seeds. At 5 pooled
seeds these become +0.79 / +0.79 / +0.90 and -0.57, -0.64.

The qualitative claims survive and one of them gets **stronger**. The low-QCLI coupling is
robustly positive in every seed at every size (per-seed minimum +0.750), which is the part
App. D says reproduces. The reversal above the paper's range now shows at **n = 8 as well**:
the median CCI of the top QCLI bin goes from 1.000 with 3 seeds to 0.074 with 5, i.e. n = 8
now also collapses onto its uniform calibration point (0.9816, 0.0000), and its pooled global ρ
falls from +0.91 to +0.08. The same bin at n = 12 and n = 16 barely moves
(0.462→0.450 and 0.472→0.529), so this is n = 8 catching up with the other two, not a new effect.
The per-seed values show why — the sign of the global ρ is genuinely
seed-dependent at n = 8 (3 of 5 positive) and n = 12 (1 of 5 positive), because whether a
trajectory reaches the degenerate uniform ceiling inside 3,333 SPSA steps is itself a
property of the seed. The mechanism App. D names — the exactly uniform distribution
maximises QCLI and has CCI = 0 — is unchanged and is what the calibration column shows.
A global ρ over a trajectory that may or may not reach the ceiling is not a stable summary,
and the ±0.92 sd at n = 8 should be reported rather than a single pooled value.
