# Seed statistics for the ML4PS-2026 tables

Every number is the mean +- sample standard deviation (ddof = 1) over seeds 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, to four significant figures. A `*` marks a cell built from fewer seeds than the 5 requested.

The seed changes model initialisation, mini-batch and restart draws, the latent prior draw, the generation draw, and the auto3 kernel's Monte-Carlo bandwidth estimate. It does NOT change the data: the training subset, the latent pool and the 200 held-out images are fixed at `R3_SPLIT_SEED` (`data_lens_r3.py:264-267`), and evaluation uses the same `CONV_SEED = 0` and the same evaluator seed for every arm.

Baselines (RBM, DCGAN) use validation-based early stopping; the IQP arms do not and run a fixed 4,000 steps. See Protocol notes at the end.

Generated by `aggregate_seeds.py`. `main.tex` is not modified by it.

Held-out set identical for every seed, on every held-out block (n_holdout=100 mean total 123.7 over seeds 0,1,2,3,4; n_holdout=200 class=axion mean total 127.3 over seeds 0,1,2,3,4; n_holdout=200 class=cdm mean total 127.1 over seeds 0,1,2,3,4; n_holdout=200 class=no_sub mean total 123.8 over seeds 0,1,2,3,4; n_holdout=200 mean total 123.9 over seeds 0,1,2,3,4) -- the split does not move with `--seed`, as `data_lens_r3.load_r3` promises.

# Part 1 -- v11 metric protocol (primary)

MMD$^2_u$ is the unbiased estimator, which -- unlike the biased one -- does not reward an arm for having fewer effective samples.

### Does early stopping change the IQP numbers by more than 1 sd?

**v10 metrics** -- 1 of the 16 (arm, N, metric) comparisons exceed 1 sd:
- IQP, learned dir. M=4 (paper kernel) N=11 PDF-JS: selected is better, and the two do not overlap (0.0009172 vs 0.0007758)

**v11 metrics (primary)** -- none. Every (arm, N, metric) comparison between the validation-selected checkpoint and the published fixed-4,000 run overlaps within 1 sd.

**Reading.** Giving the IQP arms the baselines' own early-stopping rule does not move their numbers: under the primary v11 protocol nothing separates the two, and the selected checkpoints are, if anything, marginally better. The asymmetry in the published protocol was therefore real but immaterial, which is the useful thing to be able to say -- the baselines' advantage came from being undertrained at a fixed 20,000 steps, not from being allowed to stop early.

The step counts the validation set picks for the IQP arms scatter across the whole 1k-16k range with no stable pattern (unlike the baselines, which agree seed-to-seed on 80k-160k for the RBM and 5k for the DCGAN). That is itself the finding: the IQP validation curve is flat, so there is no well-defined stopping point to find, and 4,000 steps was already on the plateau.

## Protocol notes

- **Early stopping is asymmetric, in the baselines' favour.** RBM and DCGAN get a step count chosen on a validation set disjoint from the held-out set. The IQP arms do not: they run a fixed 4,000 steps at every N and every seed.
- **DCGAN is not reproducible on this hardware, and the size of that is measured above.** cuDNN has no deterministic transposed-convolution kernel. The "Repeat runs of an identical configuration" table is not a designed experiment -- a few configurations happened to be trained twice under different job names -- but it puts a number on the problem: the same DCGAN configuration at the same seed lands 3-57% apart on PDF-JS and MMD$^2$, which is the same order as the gap between DCGAN and the IQP arms. A DCGAN number is therefore a draw from a distribution even before the seed is changed.
- **Nothing else in the study shows that behaviour.** Every RBM and every IQP configuration that was trained twice reproduced exactly, so their seed spreads are genuine seed variance rather than hardware noise.
- **The seed does not move the data.** The training subset, the latent pool and the 200 held-out images are fixed at `R3_SPLIT_SEED` regardless of an arm's `--seed` (`data_lens_r3.py:264-267`), asserted before every sweep.
